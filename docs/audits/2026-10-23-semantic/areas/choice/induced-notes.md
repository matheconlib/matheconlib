## Adversarial candidates rejected as documented scope

1. **Infinite menus can break IRC.** Let `α = ℕ` and rank the options by `r(none)=0`, `r(some n)=n+1`. At the infinite menu `ℕ`, every `some n` is beaten by `some (n+1)`, while `none` is beaten by `some 0`; hence there is no greatest feasible option and formal choice is `∅`. At `{0}`, `some 0` beats `none`, so choice is `{0}`. Thus `C(ℕ) ⊆ {0} ⊆ ℕ` but `C({0}) ≠ C(ℕ)`. This is an explicit, analytically checked boundary example, not a defect: Induced lines 35–37 explicitly say failure of existence yields empty choice, and IRC/path-independence theorems require finite menus/types (lines 393–403, 421–425). The example also explains why those hypotheses cannot simply be dropped.
2. **Indifference cannot be represented.** Economic weak preferences are commonly complete preorders, while `LinearOrder` adds antisymmetry and thus requires strict ranking of distinct bundles. These constructors consistently advertise *linear* preference orders (Induced lines 17–20, 39–40), and this is an intentional restriction rather than a false negative under their stated assumptions. To support indifference, a separate preorder-based maximizer correspondence over bundles would be needed; replacing `LinearOrder` locally while continuing to union all maximizers could incorrectly fuse distinct feasible bundles.
3. **An unrepresented empty fallback need not itself be feasible as a represented bundle.** For `α={a}`, represent only `{a}` and offer the empty menu; the output is `∅`, not the represented `{a}`. Induced lines 60–64 explicitly specify this fallback. The output remains a valid contracting `ChoiceFunction` and no theorem claims it always lies in the represented family.
4. **Mapped choice can reject all new alternatives.** If `e : {a} ↪ {a,b}` sends `a` to `a`, then any mapped choice on `{b}` is empty. That is precisely the declared extension convention at Map lines 29–32; arbitrary embeddings are not claimed to endow new alternatives with preferences.

These boundary examples were checked directly from the quantifiers and option tags, independently of the finite checker. They are not reportable semantic defects and do not call for correction.

## Independent finite checks and limitations

Reproducible checker: [`choice/induced/check_finite.py`](induced/check_finite.py) (Python standard library only).
It represents sets as bitmasks and orders by explicit permutations, separately compares the formal union-of-greatest-bundle-members description with a direct maximum selector, and checks the relevant choice conditions. Exact result:

```text
PASS: 109601 ranked represented families, 876808 bundle/menu cases; 192 option-order/menu cases; 4096 arbitrary contracting maps, injection and property preservation.
```

The bundle search includes every family of subsets on a three-alternative universe, including the empty family and families excluding the empty bundle, and every strict ranking of each family. The option search covers all strict rankings of four options and all menus on three alternatives. The map search covers all 4,096 contracting choice functions on three alternatives and their extension into four alternatives, including checks of contraction, exact action on embedded menus, exclusion of the new alternative, injectivity, and preservation of unit demand, IRC, substitutability, and finite law of aggregate demand. These are bounded checks; the general equivalence arguments above are analytic.
