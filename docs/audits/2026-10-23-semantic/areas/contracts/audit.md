## Associated theorem checks

* `Basic.lean:218–293`: the singleton blocking characterization expands exactly to a new contract accepted by its two endpoints. Shrinking a proposed blocking menu preserves each chosen contract under substitutes, so pairwise stability implies set stability without needing IRC. The reverse is immediate.
* `StableFixedPoints.lean:160–217, 315–504`: checked both round trips. At a fixed point, chosen contracts lie in the coordinate intersection; IRC transfers choice to the intersection and yields individual rationality. Substitutes+IRC give path independence, preserving candidate acceptance after replacing the menu by its chosen outcome. This reconstructs the entire state, proves no singleton block, and then set nonblocking. Conversely, stable-outcome singleton acceptance rules reconstruct a fixed canonical state. The argument applies to arbitrary types under the stronger arbitrary-superset substitutes assumption actually used.
* `StableFixedPoints.lean:514–559`, `StableOrder.lean:75–294, 400–543`: greatest/least fixed points are stable, with the correct seller/buyer choice-order extrema. The order dictionary is `p <= q` iff sellers choose outcome(q) from the union, iff buyers choose outcome(p). No unproved bridge to primitive preference welfare is stated.
* `RuralHospitals.lean:53–168`: the counting core explicitly assumes finite outcomes. Opposite choice comparisons yield per-agent weak cardinality inequalities; finite buyer/seller fiber sums squeeze totals to equality and force every inequality to equality. The public theorem assumes finite X, substitutes and finite-menu size monotonicity, deriving IRC. Conclusion is contract count, matching its statement.
* `FixedPointConvergence.lean:46–101`: finite-X monotone iteration from the top/bottom reaches greatest/least fixed points, respectively. Theorems assert existence of a stopping time; they do not claim an executable algorithm on arbitrary types or a convergence bound. Adding IRC only supplies the stable endpoint interpretation.
* `ManyToOne.lean:53–104`, `WeakSubstitutes.lean:80–133`, `Completion.lean:81–175`: unit-demand seller choices make every individually rational outcome seller-feasible. Completion agreement on feasible choices transfers rationality. Original IRC plus IRC/substitutes of the same completion support the stated one-way stability transfer. Finite-valued or finite-X assumptions are retained where the completion construction needs them. See the delegated completion note for the independent derivation.

## Rejected concerns and explicit boundary witnesses

These are controls of hypotheses and documented conventions, **not reportable defects**. Each witness is checked analytically here and directly in the preserved Python evidence where finite.

**B1 — support is weaker than locality.** At `Basic.lean:85–97`, take two contracts a,b with different buyers 0,1; a single seller suffices. Let buyer 0 choose {a} only from {a,b}, and empty otherwise; let other agents choose empty. Choices are contractive and supported, but C0({a,b})={a} differs from C0({a})=empty. Thus the bare carrier includes menu externalities. `Local` at 134–136 captures exactly the missing property, comments explicitly permit the broader carrier, and `Consistent.local` at 175–177 proves IRC eliminates this example. Proposed correction: none; the extension is intentional and named. Adding Local as a structure field would unnecessarily narrow documented models.

**B2 — pairwise versus set stability needs substitutes.** At `Basic.lean:208–214`, one buyer and one seller, two contracts a,b. Both choose {a,b} only if offered both, and choose empty on every proper menu. Empty outcome is individually rational and has no singleton block, but {a,b} blocks. Direct computation confirms the counterexample violates substitutes, so it does not refute the equivalence theorem at 283–293. Two contracts are minimal, since a nonempty blocking set on a one-contract universe is a singleton. Proposed correction: none; the required hypothesis is already present.

**B3 — infinite preference menus need existence of a best contract.** At `ManyToOne.lean:74–85`, one seller, contracts indexed by natural numbers, unemployment below each contract, and larger indices better. On the full menu every contract has a strictly preferred successor, so the selector is empty; each singleton is accepted. This defeats IRC for that infinite example. The constructor explicitly documents this behavior and only proves IRC with finite X at 102–104. Proposed correction: none for the stated API. A separate stronger constructor could require that every nonempty acceptable menu has a greatest contract if unrestricted infinite-menu maximization is desired.

**B4 — candidate acceptance differs from complement of current rejection.** At `StableFixedPoints.lean:64–70`, take one buyer, one seller, both accept every offered contract, and X={a,b}. `toSellers(empty)=toBuyers(empty)=X`, because each candidate is inserted before testing. The unique fixed point is (X,X), with stable outcome X. This independently rejects a tempting erroneous rewrite to current-menu choice or complement of current-menu rejection; the implemented insertion agrees with the cited operator.
