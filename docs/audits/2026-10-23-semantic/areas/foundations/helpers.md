| `Order/IterateFixedPoint.lean:25–36` | Any monotone or antitone natural-number sequence in a finite partial order has a consecutive repeated value. | `Order/IterateFixedPoint.lean:42–61` | Iterating a monotone self-map of a **finite complete lattice** from top/bottom reaches its greatest/least fixed point. Top iterates decrease; finiteness gives equality and hence a fixed point. Every fixed point stays below all top iterates by monotonicity, so the limit is greatest. The bottom proof uses order duality. The theorem provides an existential index and does not overstate executable computation. |
| `Order/IterateFixedPoint.lean:66–72` | Every inflationary self-map on a finite partial order has a stationary iterate from any initial element. The orbit itself increases from `x ≤ f x`; monotonicity of the map between different starting states is unnecessary and is not assumed. The antichain and singleton cases are harmless. |
| `Order/IterateFiniteSet.lean:22–28,32–58` | On subsets of a finite carrier, an inflationary update either fixes the state or strictly increases cardinality. Starting from the empty set, after `card X` iterations a fixed point has been reached. At most `card X` strict increases can occur. Empty carrier gives an immediate fixed point at iteration zero. This bound does not assert that the reached fixed point is least; a merely inflationary map need not be monotone. |
| `Quasiconvex.lean:31–46` | Quasiconcavity on a subset means convexity of all upper contour sets within that subset. Two subtype extensions agree at every point of that subset by injectivity of `Subtype.val`, hence their upper contour sets agree for every threshold. Values outside the subset are excluded by the intersection condition. The statement preserves the original `QuasiconcaveOn` semantics in both directions and does not claim quasiconcavity on the whole ambient space. |

## Hostile edge-case checks and rejected defects

1. **Nonunique relation rows.** Take one first-coordinate element and two related second-coordinate elements. `relationValue` picks one and does not encode both, but `ofRelation` cannot be formed because its row-uniqueness hypothesis fails. This is an explicitly documented selector convention, not a false positive for a partial bijection.
2. **Infinite fibers and zero `ncard`.** For unrestricted infinite sets, natural cardinality can be zero. The matching fibers have cardinality at most one, and the fiber-counting theorem requires a finite counted set. No counterexample meets the stated assumptions.
3. **Ambient closure of fixed points.** Let the complete lattice be the subsets of `{0,1,2}` and let `f(A) = A ∪ {2}` when `{0,1} ⊆ A`, otherwise `f(A) = A`. This map is monotone. The sets `{0}` and `{1}` are fixed but their ambient union `{0,1}` is sent to `{0,1,2}`. Thus arbitrary ambient-join preservation would be false. The theorem at `FixedPointLattice.lean:30–32` explicitly assumes the ambient union is fixed, which this example violates. It is not a defect.
4. **Finite-set iteration without monotonicity.** The proof uses only the inflationary comparison between consecutive states. A potential objection that nonmonotone maps could cycle fails: a strict subset increase cannot be reversed under inflationarity, and equality makes the deterministic orbit stationary. No missing map-monotonicity assumption.
