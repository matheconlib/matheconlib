# Measure and probability foundations semantic audit

Scope: all declarations in `MathEconLib/ForMathlib/{AbsolutelyContinuous,IndepFun,MeasureTheory,ProbabilityMeasure,StochasticOrder}.lean`, source revision `1d9f8cc233cdaeee0364b51befb84feea657a4da`. All five definition-like inventory entries are covered below. No source changes.

Verdict: **no confirmed substantive semantic defect in these files**. Four constructions match their intended concepts. `LikelihoodRatioLE` matches the pointwise cross-product comparison of density representatives; it is deliberately broader than a probability-distribution order when applied to arbitrary signed functions and deliberately stronger than a representative-independent measure comparison. Those domain caveats are recorded explicitly.

## Helper-only files and complete theorem scopes

### `AbsolutelyContinuous.lean`

- `AbsolutelyContinuousOnInterval.pow`, lines 34–42: closure of real absolute continuity on an interval under natural-number powers, including power zero. Multiplication closure is valid on compact intervals because absolutely continuous functions are continuous and bounded. The underlying mathlib interval notion uses the unordered interval, so reversed endpoints do not invalidate the statement.
- `ae_restrict_rnDeriv_eq_zero`, lines 46–54: under `μ≪ν`, sigma-finite `ν`, and existence of the specified Lebesgue decomposition, measurable `S` with `μ(S)=0` has density zero `ν`-a.e. on `S`. The conclusion is stated for `(rnDeriv μ ν).toReal`; the proof actually obtains the stronger `ENNReal` density equality to zero from the vanishing nonnegative integral. Thus the fact that `∞.toReal=0` creates no false positive under the premises.
- `absolutelyContinuousOnInterval_cdf`, lines 58–87: the CDF of a probability measure absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure is absolutely continuous on any interval when `cdf μ a=0`. Probability finiteness makes the density integrable; the proof identifies `F(x)=∫_a^x f` including `x<a`, using monotonicity and `F(a)=0`. The initial-zero hypothesis is sufficient but unnecessary for the broader standard theorem `F(x)=F(a)+∫_a^x f`. This theorem is a sufficient result, not a definition or an asserted characterization, so the narrower hypothesis is not a semantic false negative.

### `IndepFun.lean`

- `MeasureTheory.integral_indep_swap`, lines 36–62: for independent measurable random variables `X,Y` on a probability space, jointly measurable `h`, and integrable `h(X,Y)`, the joint expectation equals expectation against an independent copy of `Y`. The independence equality of joint law with product law transfers the **given integrability** to the product measure; Fubini then justifies iterated integration. The measurability hypotheses are present. In particular, no unsupported exchange of divergent signed integrals is made.

### `MeasureTheory.lean`

- `Continuous.integrable`, lines 20–23: continuous functions into a normed additive group are integrable on compact domains for a measure finite on compact sets, with opens measurable. Compactness makes the whole measure finite and bounds the function's norm; its compact image supports strong measurability.
- `MeasurableEquiv.piSplitAt_eq_piCongrLeft_trans_piOptionEquivProd`, lines 38–44: the same coordinate split expressed through index reordering, option-product conversion, and coordinate swap. This is an implementation identity between equivalences.
- `MeasurableEquiv.trans_symm`, lines 47–50; `map_trans`, lines 53–57; `prodComm_symm`, lines 60–63: inverse of composition, functoriality of pushforward along measurable equivalences, and involutivity of swap, respectively. Measurability is inherent in the equivalence arguments.
- `MeasurableEquiv.prodComm_prod`, lines 66–70: swapping an `SFinite` product measure swaps its marginal factors.
- `MeasureTheory.Measure.pi_map_piSplitAt`, lines 72–83: for a finite family of sigma-finite measures, inserting one coordinate into the residual independent product pushes the binary product back to the full independent product. No correlation is silently introduced, and the source factor is the correct distinguished `μ i`.

### `ProbabilityMeasure.lean`

- `measurableEmbedding_toMeasure`, lines 24–26, and `measurable_toMeasure`, lines 29–31: inclusion of the measurable subtype of probability measures into measures is a measurable embedding/map.
- `measurable_map`, lines 33–37: pushing a varying law along a **fixed measurable** function is measurable.
- `measurable_pi`, lines 39–49: forming a finite independent product of varying probability measures is measurable; the finite-index hypothesis is explicit.
- `measurable_join`, lines 64–69: the Giry barycenter construction is measurable.

- `toWeakDualContinuousMap_injective`, lines 123–128; `toWeakDualContinuousMap_isClosedEmbedding`, lines 130–133: probability measures on a compact metrizable Borel space are determined by continuous-function integrals, and their compact image is a closed embedded subset of the weak dual. ### `StochasticOrder.lean`

- `LikelihoodRatioLE.refl`, lines 42–43; `const_mul`, lines 46–54; `of_monotone_mul`, lines 57–64: the first two are determinant identities/inequalities, with nonnegative scaling hypotheses supplied. The last assumes nonnegative `p` and monotone `r`, sufficient even if `r` is signed; a probability interpretation of `r·p` would separately require it to be a density.
- `LikelihoodRatioLE.sub_mul_sub_nonneg`, lines 69–83: for monotone `g`, `(g(x)-g(y))(q(x)p(y)-p(x)q(y))≥0`. The sign is correct in both possible orderings of `x,y`; it follows from the raw determinant inequality, even for signed inputs.
- `integral_mul_integral_le_of_likelihoodRatioLE`, lines 87–137: integrating that nonnegative expression over `μ×μ` gives twice the difference between the claimed two products of integrals. All four factor integrability assumptions are explicit; sigma-finiteness supports the product/Fubini steps. **Nonnegativity of `p,q` is not needed for this algebraic theorem.**
- `integral_mul_le_of_likelihoodRatioLE`, lines 141–151: adds both total integrals equal to one and cancels them. Again valid for signed inputs as an integral inequality; its description as probability expectations presupposes density validity, which the theorem itself does not certify.

- `setIntegral_Ioi_mul_integral_le_of_likelihoodRatioLE`, lines 166–187: specializes the previous inequality to that indicator, including atoms at the threshold via the explicitly chosen open upper tail `(t,∞)`. The restriction/integrability hypotheses needed for this specialization are present.

## Adversarial candidates, explicit witnesses, and disposition

These were investigated and independently checked. They are **not** reported as substantive defects against the documented formal scope.

1. **Changing a density on one null point can destroy the pointwise LR predicate.** Under Lebesgue measure let `p=1_[0,1]`, and let `q=p` except `q(0)=2`. Both represent exactly the same uniform law, so the laws are LR-equal. At `x=0,y=1/2`, the definition requires `2·1≤1·1`, which fails. For `p,p`, all comparisons hold. Therefore a measure-level LR condition cannot be equivalent to this predicate on arbitrary supplied representatives. **Disposition:** lines 34–37 explicitly disclose this difference. There is no unacknowledged false negative. A separate measure-level API can quantify over versions of densities, or use the determinant criterion on ordered measurable sets.

2. **Signed functions can satisfy the raw relation without being densities.** The constant pair `p=q=-1` satisfies the predicate by equality but is not a density. A normalized finite example, using counting measure on ordered atoms `0<1`, is `p=(-1,2)`, `q=(-2,3)`. Both integrals are one, and the only nontrivial comparison is `(-2)·2=-4≤-3=3·(-1)`. Their integrals against `g(x)=x` are respectively `2` and `3`, so the stated expectation inequality remains true. **Disposition:** the primitive has the broader raw real-function domain and does not establish density validity. Treat this as an API domain caveat, not a false theorem. A probability-facing wrapper should require nonnegativity, measurability/integrability, and mass one, or take actual probability measures together with their density representations.

3. **The unrestricted predicate is not a transitive order.** Take `p=1_{ {1} }`, `z=0`, `r=1_{ {0} }` as functions on `ℝ`. Both `LR(p,z)` and `LR(z,r)` hold because all compared products vanish. `LR(p,r)` fails at `(0,1)` because it requires `1≤0`. **Disposition:** no transitivity theorem or order structure is asserted on all real functions. This is another reason to preserve the domain caveat; it does not invalidate the density criterion for nonzero probability laws.

4. **Requiring `dQ/dP` alone would exclude legitimate LR-ordered disjoint supports.** Let `P=δ_0,Q=δ_1`, and use the common dominating measure `P+Q`. The pointwise representatives `p=1_{ {0} },q=1_{ {1} }` satisfy the predicate: the sole potentially nonzero forward comparison is `0≤1`. Yet `Q` is not absolutely continuous with respect to `P`. **Disposition:** this concerns the informal suggestion at `StochasticOrder.lean:35–37`, not its formal predicate. If that explanatory sentence is improved, say to use versions of the two densities with respect to the common measure `P+Q`, equivalently monotonicity of `dQ/d(P+Q)` on the combined support; do not promise unrestricted equivalence to the existence of `dQ/dP`.
