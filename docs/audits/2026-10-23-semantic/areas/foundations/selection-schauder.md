# Semantic audit: Schauder infrastructure

**Verdict: no substantive conceptual defect identified.** Read-only semantic review of `MathEconLib/ForMathlib/Schauder.lean` (4,294 lines), with all 9 inventory definitions and all 171 top-level declaration signatures inspected and accounted for in `selection-schauder-declarations.json`. Proofs were inspected at the concept translations and fixed-point reductions; this does not claim a fresh line-by-line proof certification. This is a semantic review, not a new successful Lean compilation. No library source was changed.

The intended combinatorial terminology was checked against the source cited by the module, [Ivanov, *Beyond Sperner's lemma*, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/1902.00827v2): dominance excludes a point improving all order-minima; empty sets use the explicitly stated dominance convention. Rooms and doors have cardinal differences zero and one. The relevant graph consists of colorful rooms and nearly colorful rooms/doors of one distinguished type. These are combinatorial concepts; “dominance” here is not an economic Pareto-dominance predicate.

## Adversarial candidates independently rejected

An independent reviewer (`independent_schauder`) read the definitions and public theorems and separately derived the equivalences above. The following plausible concerns were explicitly checked rather than silently ignored.

1. **Apparent reversal of dominance.** With a single order and a singleton set `{x}`, the formal condition says no `y` is strictly above `x`, so `x` is that order's maximum. That is the correct Scarf dominance convention. Replacing the minimum by a maximum, reversing the strict comparison, or swapping `∃i∀x` to `∀x∃i` would change the intended concept.
2. **External color in a nearly colorful room.** Minimal example: two colors `0,1`, singleton `T={a}`, `c(a)=1`, `σ={a}`, `C={0}`, `i₀=0`. This is a dominant nearly colorful room, despite `c(σ)⊈C`; its missing color is exactly `0`. The intended definition of near-colorfulness measures `C\c(σ)`, not symmetric difference, so this is not a false positive. For doors, the cardinal condition forces the stronger inclusion and injective coloring; that inferred property is also checked by the exact finite experiment.
3. **Zero denominator.** Lean's division convention makes `DiscreteSimplex n 0` empty: all coordinates must be zero, contrary to sum one. A mesh `1/0` is outside the intended grid domain. All nonemptiness and numerator-encoding uses assume `0<l`, and Brouwer selects denominator `l+1` at lines 3961–3962. No existence assertion accidentally relies on a zero mesh.
4. **Raw adjacency does not itself enforce cellhood.** For `T={0<1}`, one color, the pairs `({0},{0})` and `(∅,{0})` satisfy structural adjacency even though the first fails dominance at `y=1`. This is not a defect in the graph: its endpoints are explicitly restricted to `IsScarfVtx` at lines 2833–2836, excluding that pair. Similarly, vacuous dominance for `T=Empty,C=∅` is outside the cell domain and excluded by `C.Nonempty`.

These are rejected defect candidates; no correction is proposed for them. Requiring `σ.Nonempty` inside dominance or requiring color-image inclusion for nearly colorful rooms would break the intended combinatorial construction.

## Reproducible verification and limits

`selection-schauder-check.py` independently translates the literal dominance quantifiers, compares them with the minimum-based concept, and constructs the actual distinguished-type graph for every order family and coloring with `|T|=1..3` and `|I|=1..3`. It checks the minimum-surjection property, colorful-room existence, all graph degrees, and door image/injectivity consequences. It also checks cyclic lexicographic compatibility and the geometric residual-mass/diameter inequalities for all subsets of simplex grids in dimensions 0–2 and positive denominators 1–4 using integer arithmetic.

This is bounded evidence only. The general definition equivalences are the mathematical arguments above; finite checks are not presented as a proof of the unbounded fixed-point theorems.
