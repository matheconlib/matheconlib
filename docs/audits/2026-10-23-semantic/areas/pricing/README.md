# Pricing and statistics semantic audit

Source revision: `1d9f8cc233cdaeee0364b51befb84feea657a4da`.

**Verdict:** no substantive semantic defect found in this area. The public definitions include explicitly documented total extensions of partial economic quantities; their interpretation conditions must be retained. The checked theorems do retain or derive the conditions they use. This is an assessment of the stated models and claims, not a claim that these definitions cover all of pricing theory.

The standard continuous virtual-value interpretation used here follows [Myerson (1981), *Optimal Auction Design*, §§2, 4–5](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spring09/cos444/papers/myerson81.pdf): private values have continuous, positive densities on bounded intervals, and incentive compatibility yields the envelope payment identity. The present code deliberately extends the probability-law representation beyond those original assumptions, and applies density-based identities only after adding absolute continuity. The treatment of nonintegrable functions follows the [Mathlib integral convention](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathematics_in_lean/mathematics_in_lean.pdf), under which the Bochner integral is zero outside its integrability domain.

## Adversarial witnesses considered and rejected

These are counterexamples to tempting **broader readings**, not to the source's stated contracts, so none is a reportable defect requiring a source correction.

1. **Atomic demand:** take `D=δ₁`, price `p=1`. Then formal demand is `1`, while `1-F(1)=0`. This would refute an unqualified strict-survival formula. `Demand.lean:74–108` explicitly proves the correct `1-F(p)+D{p}` identity and adds the required no-atom hypothesis before dropping the last term. `checks.py` independently checks the atom identity and direct seller accounting on finite laws with exact rational weights.

2. **RN density on a singular law:** for `D=δ₁`, the AC component relative to Lebesgue measure is zero, so the RN derivative is zero AE and its integral is zero rather than one. This would refute treating `realDensity` as every law's full PDF. `Density.lean:18–27,38–51` explicitly disclaims that interpretation, and all whole-law integration rewrites supply AC. The correction needed for the broader reading—requiring AC—is already present in the theorems.

3. **A density changed at a singleton:** for `U[0,1]`, choose the usual density but change its value to zero at `v=1/4`. It remains an AE density, yet the quotient `virtualValue` at that point becomes zero instead of the continuous representative's `-1/2`. This would refute representative-independent pointwise virtual values. No such invariance is claimed. AE identities use only AE density agreement; first-order conditions require `HasDerivAt cdf (density price) price` at the candidate (`PostedPriceFirstOrder.lean:53,84,118`), which this changed density fails at `1/4`. Nonzero division is explicitly guarded (`VirtualValue.lean:56–62`).

4. **Nonintegrable expected payment:** let `D=U[0,1]`, zero allocation, identity strategy on real actions, and payment `t(v)=1/v` for `v>0`, zero otherwise. The positive economic expectation is infinite, while the real Bochner totalization is zero. This establishes why the primitive alone cannot certify an ordinary expectation. The source explicitly says so at `SingleParameter.lean:19–29`; it never claims that all payments have finite expectation. The bounded-support envelope-IC payment theorems cannot admit this example because an always-zero allocation under envelope IC has constant utility and hence constant on-strategy payment. No correction to the stated extension is needed.

5. **Nonunique prices outside support:** for `U[0,1]` with continuation `r=2`, price `1` and every price `p>1` produce no sale and seller payoff `2-c`. Global uniqueness would fail. The optimum and uniqueness claims expressly quantify over `[0,1]` (`UniformPricing.lean:18–26,174–200`), on which only `p=1` produces this maximum. The proposed broader conclusion is intentionally absent.

6. **Zero derivative versus optimum:** a zero first-order expression alone does not imply local or global optimality for a general nonregular distribution. `PostedPriceFirstOrder.lean:74–78` explicitly states only necessity; sufficient global conditions are separate sign hypotheses in `PostedPriceOptimality.lean:344–374`. No regularity or ironing predicate is defined in these modules, and no unproved general regular-distribution theorem is advertised.

The general reason the tail formula tolerates atoms is that the inner integral uses Lebesgue measure in cutoff `t`: for a fixed value `v`, `∫_a^v q(t)dt=∫_a^b q(t)1[t≤v]dt`. Taking expectation and exchanging these finite/nonnegative integrals gives the closed tail exactly. Whether a cutoff singleton is included changes no Lebesgue integral. The order-statistic law separately excludes atoms through its declared PDF assumptions.

## Four audit passes and verification evidence

1. **Concept extraction:** read every source module and `docs/PricingArchitecture.md`; mapped all 17 inventoried definitions to their standard concepts and interpretation conditions.
2. **Hostile case construction:** tested atoms at the cutoff, singular laws, arbitrary density representatives, zero densities, nonintegrable payments, negative support/price values, degenerate and reversed intervals, off-support strategies, endpoint optima, and no-sale prices. The six rejected broader-reading witnesses above retain the decisive reasons they are not defects.
3. **Independent verification:** separate reviewers audited the entire order-statistics module (`orderstats.md`) and challenged the distribution/pricing assessments (`independent.md`). No reportable counterexample remained. The independent reviewer notes give their own calculations and hypotheses.
4. **Reconciliation and exact checks:** all 17 inventory rows appear above; all 14 source files appear in the tables. `python3 docs/audits/2026-10-23-semantic/areas/pricing/checks.py` succeeded with **880 atomic demand/accounting cases, 320 atomic tail-integral cases, and 850 uniform optimum/uniqueness/derivative cases**, all exact rational arithmetic. These checks validate the encoded finite instances only; the general arguments are stated above and in the independent notes. No Lean compilation is claimed by this area audit; no dependencies were installed and no library source was edited.

No proposed source patch is supplied because no substantive defect survived the stated-scope and independent checks. The expected-value and quotient caveats should remain visible in future API documentation and theorem statements.
